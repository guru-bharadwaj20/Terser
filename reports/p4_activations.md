# Phase 4 activation study (row 4.2.1)

NumPy MLP (`terser.models.neural.numpy_mlp`) on the row 3.1.3 pair table
(`data/processed/p4_pairs.parquet`, 14,732 train pairs, 27 features), one grouped train / val
split, 30 epochs, 5 seeds per activation. Generated by
`python -m terser.phase4.nn_studies activations --table data/processed/p4_pairs.parquet`;
per-run numbers in `reports/p4_activations_runs.csv`.

| activation | final val AUC | best val AUC | epochs to plateau | saturated units | dead units | grad norm L1 / L2 / L3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| step (STE) | 0.771 ± 0.006 | 0.778 | 17.6 ± 3.2 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.355 / 0.308 / 0.149 |
| sigmoid | 0.771 ± 0.002 | 0.771 | 14.0 ± 3.7 | 0.001 | 0.000 | 0.063 / 0.111 / 0.110 |
| tanh | 0.771 ± 0.008 | 0.782 | 14.6 ± 3.1 | 0.014 | 0.000 | 0.330 / 0.329 / 0.127 |
| ReLU | 0.760 ± 0.010 | 0.775 | 4.4 ± 1.1 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.363 / 0.489 / 0.186 |

## Interpretation

* **Sigmoid** has the smallest first-layer gradient (0.063, ~5x below the others). Its derivative
  is at most 0.25, so each layer shrinks the backward signal and the input layer learns slowest.
  Almost no units saturate here (0.1%): the shrinkage comes from the 0.25 ceiling, not from
  saturation, and on 27 standardised features with two hidden layers it costs nothing in final AUC.
* **tanh** keeps a ~4x larger gradient (derivative 1 at 0) and reaches the best peak AUC (0.782),
  but 1.4% of units saturate and the run-to-run spread is the widest of the smooth activations.
* **ReLU** plateaus fastest (4.4 epochs): the unit derivative on the active side passes gradient
  unchanged, so the largest gradient norms reach every layer. It then overfits. Final AUC
  (0.760) sits ~0.015 below its own peak, the largest drop of the four. No dead units were
  observed at this learning rate and depth.
* **Step with a straight-through estimator** matches the smooth activations. The STE backward pass
  is the identity, so the gradient norms look like ReLU's while the forward pass is binary; the
  binarised hidden layer acts as a regulariser, so the run plateaus late (17.6 epochs) but keeps
  its AUC.

All four land within 0.011 AUC of each other, so on this table the choice of activation mainly
changes **convergence speed** (ReLU fastest), not the final quality-gate score. Early stopping on
the grouped val fold matters more than the activation itself.
