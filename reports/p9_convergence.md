**C9.1 at the plan budget (30,000 episodes): fail** (most settled learner: max update 0.164, net change 0.146). With 200,000 episodes: pass. **C9.1 (extended run): pass**.

**Reading.** The Q-learner does log the table change (`QLearner.max_delta_`, the largest |dQ| per episode), and the reruns reproduce the 9.2.2 sweep exactly (the plan-budget rows equal `reports/p9_qlearning.md`), so the rerun learners are the same tables as the study's. At the plan budget the criterion is not met on either statistic. The 9.2.3 policy learner keeps epsilon >= 0.05, so it keeps exploring and moves by up to 19.5 per window. The settled learner (epsilon annealed to 0, step 1 / N(s, a)) shrinks the change about as 1 / N, but the state is aliased: one (state, action) pair covers many prompts whose STOP rewards range from about +40 to about -100, so each update moves Q by (reward spread) / N. Even at 200,000 episodes the window maximum is 0.0582, and it is not monotone (it jumps whenever a rarely visited pair is updated). Reaching 1e-3 against a spread of about 140 needs on the order of 10^5 visits per pair; a richer state (less aliasing) or a far longer run is needed for the original 1e-3 target.
